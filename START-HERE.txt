WHAT CHANGED IN THIS ROUND
================================================================
All seven of these came from you using the site on its first day.

1. THE CHAT WINDOW WAS TOO SMALL TO READ
   370x520 with 13.5px text. Now 412 wide, up to 640 tall, 14.5px text,
   a shorter header and messages running wider before they wrap. A phone
   was already full-screen and is unchanged.

2. "Hello, any employment" REACHED THE DESK AS ORDINARY SUPPORT
   "employment" was missing from the "any vacancies / any job / any
   openings" list, and on its own it was one weak word against a
   threshold of three.

   Adding that one word would have fixed that one sentence. Instead
   there is now a rule that catches by SHAPE: a short message that names
   work and names nothing a customer would ask about. So the next
   phrasing nobody thought of is caught too.

   Two more found while testing:
     * "Any openings today?" in a boutique means "are you open today" —
       the person is standing up to come in. No longer treated as a job
       enquiry. "Any openings for a sales assistant" still is.
     * "Piece work" is how casual labour is asked for here and was going
       through. Now caught — while "a piece", which is what this shop
       calls its products, still is not.

3. THE SHOP WAS COUNTING ITSELF
   The admin page was never tracked, but you walking through your own
   shop front was — and you are the one browsing most days. Signing in
   to the admin now marks that device and Analytics skips it.

   Per browser, and it stays: a phone used to run the shop is a shop
   phone. To count a device again, clear that browser's site data for
   the website.

   NOTE: it takes effect on a device the next time somebody signs in to
   the admin there. Figures already recorded still include your visits.

4. THE QUICK VIEW WAS NOT COUNTED AS LOOKING AT A PIECE
   product_view only fired when a piece was opened on its own page.
   Opening the quick view — which has a buy button in it — recorded
   nothing. That is exactly why a piece showed "0 views, 1 to cart".

5. "/" READ AS A STRAY MARK
   Most viewed pages now says Home page, Shop, Policies, and
   "Piece — SKU". Anything else is left exactly as recorded.

6. SOMEBODY WHO LEFT LINGERED FOR FIVE MINUTES
   A tab beats once a minute while it is being looked at, so the "here
   now" count now clears two minutes after the last beat instead of
   five. Nobody still reading is dropped; somebody who closed the tab is
   gone in about two minutes.

7. NOT CHANGED, AND WHY
   * Duplicates: already correct. A visitor id lives in the browser and
     a visit id lasts a session with a 30-minute idle window, so one
     person reloading ten times is one visit and one visitor.
   * Returning at 0%: correct. "Returning" means seen on an EARLIER day.
     Recording is a day old, so everyone is legitimately new. Returning
     visitors appear on their own from tomorrow.


================================================================
8. YOU CAN NOW START THE TRAFFIC COUNT AGAIN
   Settings > System & Maintenance > "Start the traffic count again".
   Throws away every visit the website has recorded, so the figures can
   be real customers only after you have finished testing.

   Offered to the SHOP OWNER alone, and the database refuses anybody
   else even if the button were reached another way. You have to type
   your password.

   IT CLEARS TRAFFIC AND NOTHING ELSE. Your orders, customers,
   subscribers, reviews and conversations are not analytics and are not
   touched. Only the visit counts go, and it cannot be undone. Counting
   starts again with the next visitor.

   This is the one to use on your current figures, which still include
   your own testing visits from before the change above.

9. ADD TO CART WHEN A CUSTOMER REMOVES IT AGAIN
   Left as it is, deliberately. It stays counted: nine people putting a
   piece in a basket is a real signal about that piece even if four
   later changed their minds. Say the word if you would rather see
   added / removed / net.
